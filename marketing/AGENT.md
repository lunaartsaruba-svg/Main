## Target Audience

**Ideal customer:** The Sensory Product Buyer, The Workshop Attendee

**Full descriptor:** Based on what Luna Arts sells and how it sells, you actually have two ideal customer profiles that overlap but aren't identical.
The Sensory Product Buyer
This is a parent or caregiver, likely mother, age 28 to 45, managing a child with sensory processing needs, ADHD, anxiety, or autism, though plenty of neurotypical kids and adults buy for stress relief too. She's active on Instagram and TikTok, price sensitive but willing to pay for something that visibly works (your butter squishies selling out fast at AFL 18 tells you this). She buys on impulse when she sees a satisfying video of the product being squished, popped, or clicked. She's local Aruban or expat, shops at pop-ups, markets, or through WhatsApp orders as much as a storefront.
There's a second sensory buyer worth naming separately: the adult self-soother. Someone managing their own anxiety, a fidget-at-the-desk office worker, a psychology-adjacent audience (your own field gives you real credibility here) who buys keyboard clickers and fidgets for themselves, not a kid. This buyer responds to language about focus, calm, and regulation rather than "kids toys."
The Workshop Attendee
This is a different person. Usually a woman, 25 to 55, looking for a creative outlet or a wellness moment, not necessarily "artistic." She's drawn to intentional, guided experiences, your Mindful Art Series vision board workshop is a good example of what she wants: structure plus permission to slow down. She values aesthetic and atmosphere (teal and gold, calm lighting, a sense of care) as much as the craft itself. She's likely to book with a friend or as a small group outing, and she's a strong candidate to become a repeat customer if the experience feels personal rather than generic.
Where they overlap
Both buyers respond to sensory experience, calm/regulation language, and a brand that feels warm and intentional rather than clinical. Your Catholic and psychology background actually gives you an edge here that's worth leaning into more directly, since "sensory tools for calm and focus" sits naturally next to your own credibility in mental wellbeing, without needing to be explicitly religious or clinical in the marketing itself.
If it's useful, I can turn this into a proper one-page customer persona document, or draft social captions or ad targeting specifically aimed at one of these segments.
